Kids: Learn Tamil by Playing

A Tamil friend who lives on the screen. Feed them, dress them, grow a garden of letters — and learn all 247 Tamil characters and a thousand everyday Tamil words without ever being told you are in a lesson.

In development Android

Overview

A Flutter app for Tamil children aged roughly 4–8, built on a simple idea: a child will come back for a friend, not for a curriculum. The app opens on a companion the child picks — a girl or a boy — who stands in a painted scene, blinks, gets hungry, gets sleepy, and reacts to being touched. Everything the app teaches is bought with what that friend earns.

Behind the character sit three systems that do the actual teaching. Tamil-247 walks every one of Tamil's 247 characters along a snake map, and each one is written freehand from memory on a blank canvas — no tracing tube, no dotted outline — and marked on shape against real stroke data, with a pass mark measured rather than guessed. Ten word games draw on a roster of over a thousand Tamil words. And a garden turns each learned character into a sunflower that grows three different ways, so a child who does not want to write today can still make something happen.

The word roster is the part that took the longest to get right. Every word is weighted by how well a Tamil child actually knows it, so roughly three-quarters of what a child meets is something already in the room with them — அம்மா, மாடு, தோசை, பள்ளி — while the rarer words stay reachable instead of being locked away. English loanwords written in Tamil script were pulled out and replaced with the everyday Tamil where one honestly exists: பஸ் became பேருந்து, டீ became தேநீர், ரப்பர் became அழிப்பான். Where no Tamil word is genuinely spoken — கார், பென்சில் — the loanword stays, because teaching a child a coined word nobody at home says is not teaching them Tamil.

The whole app is built child-safety-first: no login, no account, no chat, no text field anywhere a child could type into, and no analytics or crash-reporting SDK. Every coin, letter and plant lives in local storage on the device. Ads, outbound links and quitting all sit behind a parental gate whose sum replaces itself on a wrong answer, so a child cannot get through it by elimination.
