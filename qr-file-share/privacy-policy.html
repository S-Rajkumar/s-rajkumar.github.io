Privacy Policy

QR File Share — Last updated: September 1, 2026

Overview

QR File Share ("the app") is a peer-to-peer file transfer tool that lets you send files, folders, and text between devices connected to the same local Wi-Fi network or mobile hotspot. One device shows a QR code and the other scans it to pair; once paired, either device can send or receive. This policy explains what data the app accesses, how it is used, and what is — and is not — collected.

Unless a section says otherwise, everything below describes the Android app. The Windows version behaves differently in a few important ways — see The Windows version near the end.

QR File Share does not use the internet or any cloud server to transfer your files. All file and text transfers happen directly between your devices over your local network. We do not have access to, store, or see the content you share. (The advertising SDK described further down does contact Google's servers — but it never has access to your files.)

Permissions the app requests

Permission Why it's needed
Camera To scan the pairing QR code displayed by the other device. The camera is used only while that scanning screen is open, and no image is stored or transmitted.
Wi-Fi state To read your device's local IP address, which is what the pairing QR code encodes so the other device knows where to connect.
Internet Runs the small local transfer server on your device and connects to the other device's server. It is also used by the advertising SDK described below, which does contact Google's servers. Your files themselves never leave your local network.
Storage (older Android only) On Android 10 and earlier, the system requires a storage permission to write received files. On Android 11 and later the app does not request any broad storage permission at all — see below.

How the app accesses your files

The app deliberately avoids broad storage access. It never requests "All files access", and on modern Android it holds no general storage permission:

  • Choosing individual files uses the system file picker. Only the files you pick are handed to the app.
  • Choosing a folder uses Android's Storage Access Framework. You explicitly grant one folder, and the app can read only inside that folder.
  • Saving received files goes through Android's MediaStore into your public Downloads folder, which needs no special permission.

What data we collect

QR File Share does not collect, transmit, or store any personal data, file contents, or usage analytics on external servers. Files you choose to share are transferred directly between your device and the receiving device over your local network only, for the duration of the active session.

Security of transfers — please read

Transfers happen over plain HTTP on your local network. They are not encrypted, and there is no password on a transfer session. This is a deliberate trade-off that keeps pairing instant and works without any account or internet connection, but it has a real consequence worth understanding:

While a transfer session is active, anyone else on the same Wi-Fi network who knows your device's address and port could download the files you are currently offering. Only use the app on networks you trust, such as your home Wi-Fi or your own hotspot — and disconnect when you are finished.

Nothing is offered before you pick it, and nothing remains available once you tap STOP or close the app. The app never exposes your wider file system — only the specific files or folder you chose for that session.

Clipboard access

When you use the text-sharing feature, the app may read text you have copied to generate a QR code, and may write scanned text back to your clipboard on the receiving device. This clipboard data is never sent anywhere outside your device and is not stored by the app.

Advertising

This app displays banner advertisements served through Google AdMob. To do so, AdMob collects your device's advertising ID along with certain device and usage information, and shares it with Google for advertising and analytics purposes, in accordance with Google's Privacy Policy. This is the only data associated with the app that leaves your device. You can reset the advertising ID or limit ad personalisation at any time in your device's Google settings.

The Windows version

A Windows build is available to download from the developer's site. It is the same app, but several sections above simply do not apply to it:

  • No advertising. The Windows build contains no ad SDK and shows no ads, so no advertising ID is collected and nothing is sent to Google.
  • No permissions are requested. There is no camera, storage, or Wi-Fi permission prompt on Windows.
  • No camera. Windows cannot scan QR codes, so it always displays the pairing code for a phone to scan, or you type the other device's address by hand.
  • Received files are written straight to a folder you choose, rather than through Android's MediaStore.

With no ads and no analytics, the Windows build sends nothing at all off your device. Everything in the "Security of transfers" section above still applies: the transfer itself is unencrypted, so use it only on networks you trust.

The download is not code-signed, so Windows may warn that the publisher is unknown. That is expected for an independently distributed app.

Data sharing with third parties

Other than the advertising SDK mentioned above, QR File Share does not share any data with third parties. There are no analytics trackers, no account system, and no remote database associated with this app.

Children's privacy

This app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from children.
